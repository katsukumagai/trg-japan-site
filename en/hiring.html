How not to lose good candidates

When hiring stalls, the cause is often the process, not the size of the candidate pool.

A slow process

Strong candidates run several processes at once. If your interviews are spaced out, another company closes first.

DO INSTEADFix the number of rounds and the timeline up front, and hold the slots early

A vague job description

"Drive data utilization" does not let candidates see how their experience connects.

DO INSTEADState the work for the first six months, the team structure and the stack

Revealing the salary range late

Learning after the final interview that expectations do not match wastes everyone's time.

DO INSTEADCompare with the salary table above and fix the range before the first interview

Interviewers using different criteria

Inconsistent evaluation delays decisions and leaves a poor impression.

DO INSTEADAlign every interviewer on must-haves and evaluation points before the first round

Four common mistakes when entering Japan

Early hiring involves decisions that are hard to reverse later.

Searching with an English-only job post

Japanese-first candidates and bilingual candidates are different pools with different expectations.

DO INSTEADPrepare the same job description in both Japanese and English

Applying the HQ pay scale as is

In Japan, bonus, allowances and severance practice are how candidates compare offers.

DO INSTEADCheck the market in the salary table and present total compensation

Long HQ interview chains with no local authority

Time zones and multi-stage interviews that run for months lose candidates to other offers.

DO INSTEADCut the number of rounds and give the Japan side the final call

Making an offer before deciding the employer

A local company, a branch or an EOR changes the contract, the start date and where insurance is enrolled.

DO INSTEADDecide the employment route below before starting the first hire

Three ways to employ people in Japan

For incorporation and contracts, consult a judicial scrivener, tax accountant and labor and social security attorney. We do not give legal or tax advice.

1

Japanese company (KK / GK)

A Japanese legal entity becomes the employer. Requires incorporation and enrollment in social and labor insurance.

FITS WHENYou will hire several people and operate in Japan long term
2

Japan branch

Register as the Japan branch of a foreign company. A representative in Japan is required.

FITS WHENYou want to start operating in Japan under the same legal entity as HQ
3

EOR（Employer of Record）

A provider becomes the employer and handles employment, payroll and social insurance. You can hire the first person before setting up an entity.

FITS WHENYou want to test the market with one or two people and decide on incorporation later

Interviewing a country manager

Your first leader in Japan carries both business judgment and hands-on execution.

What to assess

  • Experience building customers from zero (how they won the first ten)
  • Experience managing expectations between HQ and the Japan market
  • Can explain to executives in both Japanese and English
  • Can interview and decide the first hires personally
  • Expectations on reporting line and decision authority
  • Understanding of Japanese business practice (ringi approval, referrals, partner channels)

Sample questions

  • What will you report to HQ as results in the first three months?
  • When HQ policy did not fit the local market, what did you do?
  • If you could hire only three people, which roles and in what order?
  • How would you get a first meeting with a decision-maker at a large account?
  • What do you need from HQ to take this role?
