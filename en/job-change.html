1

Write your CV

STEP 1 · CV / 職務経歴書

Screeners look for what the situation was, what you owned and what changed, not just what you did. Scale and results in numbers beat a list of tools.

AVOIDWorked on data platform development. Used SQL, Python and AWS.
BETTER (example)Designed and built a data platform processing 200M log events a month with a team of three. Cut the runtime of key queries from 40 minutes to 6. AWS (S3, Glue, Redshift), Python, SQL.

Basic structure

  • Open with a 5–8 line summary: field, years of experience, strengths
  • Group skills by area (e.g. data platform / ML / BI)
  • For each employer: role, team size, results, in that order
  • Keep it to 2–3 A4 pages

Self-check before sending

  • Do results include numbers (volume, money, time, headcount)?
  • Is your role clear (lead vs. member)?
  • Do you separate skills used in production from skills you only touched?
  • Does your wording reflect the language of the job description?
Japanese companies sometimes ask for a standard Japanese-format résumé (rirekisho) at the final stage or at offer. Check what the company requires and prepare it early.
2

Prepare for interviews

STEP 2 · INTERVIEW PREPARATION

We share company-specific questions and evaluation points in our conversations. Below is what applies to almost every company. Tap a section to open it.

Research the company and interviewers
  • Listed companies: read the earnings presentation and mid-term plan. Foreign firms and startups: the latest funding news and press releases
  • Try the product (free plan or public demo) and be ready to say what you thought in one sentence
  • Check interviewers' LinkedIn, blogs and conference talks for their interests and background
What each role type gets asked
  • Data engineer: pipeline design, how you guarantee data quality, cost vs. performance trade-offs
  • ML / LLM engineer: how you choose metrics, handling degradation in production, why RAG or fine-tuning
  • Data scientist: framing the problem, connecting analysis to a business decision, the impact you had
  • Solutions engineer / pre-sales: how you uncover customer problems, building a demo and proposal, handling technical objections
  • FDE / consultant: structuring vague requirements, working inside the customer's organization, managing scope and deadlines
Questions almost everyone gets
  • Why you are changing jobs and what you want next (frame it around what you want, not what you dislike)
  • Why this company: answer with facts about its business, product and organization
  • Your hardest project, and what you learned from something that did not work
  • Current salary and expected salary
Questions to ask them
  • What results are expected in the first 3 months and the first year?
  • How is the team structured and how are decisions made (who decides what)?
  • Why is this role open (growth, backfill, new business)?
  • How is performance evaluated, and when are raises and promotions decided?
Interviews with foreign or overseas-HQ companies
  • For English interviews, prepare a 3-minute version of your career you can say smoothly
  • Technical rounds such as live coding, case studies or system design may appear. Confirm the format in advance
  • In a new Japan entity, roles are broad. Prepare examples of how far you can act on your own
When they ask about salary
  • You do not need to fix a number in the interview itself. Talk to us beforehand about how to state your expectation
  • Be ready to explain your current pay split into base, bonus, allowances and equity
3

Offer documents and reference checks

STEP 3 · OFFER DOCUMENTS & REFERENCE CHECK

After the final interview, terms are negotiated and paperwork begins. Being unprepared here can affect your start date and terms.

Documents commonly requested

  • Latest CV and résumé
  • Most recent withholding tax slip (gensen choshuhyo)
  • Graduation certificate, if requested
  • A copy of your residence card, for non-Japanese nationals

Reference checks

  • Common at foreign firms and startups, around the offer stage
  • Referees are usually a direct manager or colleague. They must consent in advance
  • If you do not want your current employer to know, ask the company whether a former manager is acceptable
We submit documents only after you agree, company by company. We never contact your current employer or related companies.
4

Accepting the offer and resigning

STEP 4 · OFFER ACCEPTANCE & RESIGNATION

Confirm the terms in writing before you accept. Resignation is where doing things in the wrong order causes trouble.

What to confirm at the offer meeting

  • Breakdown of base salary, bonus, allowances and equity (RSU / options)
  • Probation period and its terms
  • Team, role and reporting line
  • Location, remote work and flex-time rules
  • Start date (leave room for your resignation process)

How to resign

  • Sign the offer letter (employment terms notice) before telling your employer
  • Tell your direct manager verbally first, then submit the resignation letter
  • Check your work rules. Under the Civil Code you can leave two weeks after giving notice, but many companies' rules require one to two months
  • Offer a handover plan yourself, and arrange paid leave early
If you receive a counter-offer: unless money was the only reason you looked, the same reasons remain even after a raise. Check whether those reasons are actually resolved, including promises in writing, before deciding. We can think it through with you.
